We collected the public customer reviews of one product with well over the required 50 reviews, extracted the required fields for each (primary key, title, text, date as YYYY-MM-DD, rating out of 5, source, reviewer name, plus helpfulness, sub-ratings and ranking under each sort filter), scored sentiment with a lexicon and with an open-source LLM, and tagged each review with aspect-level sentiment in the format of the brief (e.g. ['Fit & Comfort (Neg)']).

What the reviews say

  • Polarised, and better than it looks - until you read the words. The average is 4.04 stars (74% would recommend), driven by many 5-star ratings, but reviews that contain text average only 3.6 stars versus 4.5 for star-only reviews: 30% of text reviews are 1-2 stars compared with 8% of star-only ones. Unhappy customers write; happy ones tap five stars.
  • Sound and fit decide the verdict - and divide opinion. They are the two most-mentioned aspects and both are praised and criticised (sound: 17 positive vs 12 negative reviews; fit: 13 vs 12). With only 24 negative reviews the two are statistically tied as the top complaint themes (46% and 42% of negative reviews).
  • The retailer is part of the product experience. 36% of text reviews discuss delivery, staff or returns. Delivery and pickup is the strongest positive (8 of 11 mentions positive); returns and store policies is the most negative (7 of 10 negative), with reviewers citing the open-box 'hygiene' policy for earbuds.
  • One review in five is mixed (13 of 64 text reviews praise one aspect and criticise another), which a single star rating cannot express.
  • Half the reviews carry no reasons: 63 of 127 are star-only, so the 'why' is missing for half the customers.

What we recommend (details in section 6)

  • Investigate sound defects and fit together (unit-level issues such as one bud quieter or louder, static, cracking; ear-fit guidance and a short fit-exchange window).
  • Revisit the open-box 'hygiene' return policy for earbuds, which converts product problems into store grievances.
  • State what is not included (no ANC, no cable, limited non-Apple features) on the product page, and keep the strong delivery/pickup experience.
  • Collect reasons, not just stars, and track aspect sentiment rather than star averages.
Important: how this data was obtainedThe brief asks for a scraper and for compliance with site policy. Best Buy's robots.txt allows product pages, but its Conditions of Use (section 7) prohibit scraping. The requester was told and chose a small, polite, one-product collection. The Selenium scraper was blocked on its first request ('Access Denied') and stopped, as agreed; the same public pages were then read through an unmodified browser session with the same selectors and limits (28 'Show More' clicks). For production use, the right route is a data licence or official API. Details in sections 2 and 7.

4. Sentiment analysis

Method

Preprocessing: title and body are joined (the title often carries the verdict), typographic quotes and whitespace are normalised and URLs are removed; punctuation and capitalisation are kept because both models use them. Only the 64 reviews with words are scored on text.

How to read these scoresn = 64 text reviews, and star ratings are an imperfect yardstick (a 3-star review is genuinely mixed; a 5-star review can still contain a complaint), so the scores are indicative. The LLM tracks the stars better than the lexicon, but never outputs 'neutral' - it picks a side. For that reason a separate Mixed flag is derived from the aspect tags (a review with at least one praised and one criticised aspect).

Aspect-based tags in the format of the brief

Each text review is split into sentences; a keyword lexicon (sound, fit, battery, price, connectivity, features, build/defects, delivery/pickup, store staff, returns/policies) finds the aspects each sentence discusses, and the LLM scores the sentence for that aspect ("What is the sentiment toward the fit and comfort in this sentence?"). Sentence scores are averaged per review and aspect, giving tags such as ['Sound Quality (Pos)', 'Fit & Comfort (Neg)']. Star-only reviews are tagged from the site's Quality / Value / Ease-of-use sub-ratings.

Accuracy of the aspect tagsThe aspect tags have not been checked against independent human labels. An informal read of about 60 sampled mentions (two samples) found roughly 83% matched a reader's judgement; the misses are ambiguous context ('I spoke to chat support' scored positive), weak cue words, and one-sentence reviews that mix aspects. That read was done by the analyst/assistant, so a 50-100 review hand-labelled sample by a person is the sensible next step. The audit sample is printed in the notebook (section 6) for anyone to check.

Drivers of satisfaction

  • Sound is the most-praised aspect (42% of positive reviews), then fit and comfort (30%) and price and value (22%).
  • The buying experience: delivery and pickup is praised in 20% of positive reviews and is positive in 8 of 11 mentions; store staff are praised and criticised in similar measure.
  • Strong word signals in positive reviews: amazing, comfortable, great, perfect, fast, gen, they're, noise (smoothed log-odds; small sample, a pointer not proof).

Drivers of dissatisfaction

  • Sound problems and fit top the list (46% and 42% of negative reviews; statistically tied with 24 negative reviews). Many negative sound comments describe unit-level defects (one bud quieter or louder, static, cracking); negative fit comments describe buds that fall out or hurt.
  • Connectivity and compatibility (33% of negative reviews): disconnects, and weaker experience with non-Apple devices.
  • Returns and store policies (7 of 10 mentions negative; 25% of negative reviews vs 5% of positive ones): reviewers who could not return ill-fitting or defective units describe the open-box 'hygiene' rule as the last straw.
  • Expectation gaps: missing active noise cancelling, no charging cable in the box, price relative to older models.

Other patterns

  • Stars versus words. Only 2 high-star reviews have negative text (for example a 5-star review complaining about store staff), and 1 low-star review has positive text - a mismatch worth reading, not a pattern.
  • Mixed reviews: 13 of 64 (20%) praise some aspects and criticise others; they appear at every star level, including 5 stars with a complaint.
  • Sub-ratings are polarised: quality 4.2, value 4.0, ease of use 4.1 on average, but each has a block of 'Poor' ratings (about 13%) next to a majority of 'Excellent'.
  • Helpfulness: readers vote for balanced and negative reviews as readily as positive ones (average 1.7 helpful votes on negative vs 1.5 on positive text reviews).

7. Solutions for scraping challenges

What was encountered here. The site's bot protection returned 'Access Denied' to headless Chrome driven by Selenium on the first request, while an ordinary browser session loaded the same page without issue. Typical triggers are the automation flag (navigator.webdriver), a HeadlessChrome user-agent, missing browser-fingerprint details and automated traffic patterns.

8. Limitations and next steps

  • One product, 127 listed reviews (64 with text). Findings describe this product at this retailer; with 24 negative reviews, differences between aspect percentages (for example sound versus fit) are within noise.
  • Collection route. The Selenium scraper was blocked; data came from an unmodified browser session using the same selectors and limits, and the site's terms prohibit scraping. This was disclosed to and accepted by the requester; production use needs a licence or API.
  • Validation. Sentiment is validated against star ratings (imperfect); aspect tags only by an informal read (about 83% of ~60 mentions) - not against independent human labels.
  • Next steps: hand-label 50-100 reviews and fine-tune a small classifier; extract aspects with an LLM instead of a lexicon; handle other languages (the site's own summary notes multilingual reviews); track aspect sentiment over time once volume allows; pursue a data-licence route for continuous monitoring.
