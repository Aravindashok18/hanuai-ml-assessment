We analysed 1,000 warranty claims for one vehicle cohort whose infotainment system (radio / head unit, display, camera, satellite radio, Bluetooth, OnStar) is the subject of every claim. Three free-text narratives per claim (customer concern, technician diagnosis, repair performed) were converted into structured tags, the claims were grouped by type of issue, and the results were tested statistically so that only patterns that survive multiple-testing correction are reported as findings.

What the data says

  • The radio is the hub. 88% of claims end with the radio replaced - 80-94% in every symptom cluster, including camera, phone, satellite-radio and screen complaints. Customers describe many different symptoms; the repair is almost always the same.
  • Diagnosis is not converging. 46% of claims replace the radio without documenting a physical cause; 33% are escalated to technical assistance (TAC), and 92% of those end in replacement. Only 31% explicitly document an internal radio fault and just 2% show an external wiring/electrical cause.
  • One trend is statistically real. Of 32 categories tested for change over time, only one survives correction: claims where the radio stays on after the vehicle is switched off / drains the battery (46 claims) rose from 4.0% early on and 2.2% in mid-2020 to 11.1% in 2021 (adjusted p < 0.001).
  • Camera / video complaints concentrate in specific models - FusionNova (18 vs 7 expected), HyperFury X (30 vs 17 expected), StellarGlide (16 vs 7 expected) - and this is confirmed independently by the semantic clusters and by the repair-narrative topics. Plants show no significant concentration.
  • The structured codes mislead. The coded cause barely changes what technicians found or fixed, and the coded complaint 'Communication' is mostly audio drop-outs and screen blanking, not connectivity.
  • Early-life failures matter: 21% of claims arrive within 90 days of the vehicle entering service, and 76 claims are dated before the in-service date.

What we recommend (details in section 6)

  • Failure-analyse returned radios with the supplier (documented-fault, undocumented, camera-complaint and stays-on samples) - the single largest cost driver.
  • Software-first, verify-before-replace workflow and a mandatory evidence standard (fault codes, circuit tests, failure code) before a radio is authorised for replacement.
  • Contain the emerging 'stays on / battery drain' issue now: check against software releases, measure key-off current draw, add a power-down test to software validation.
  • Investigate the camera/video signal path on the over-represented model lines before it spreads.
  • Fix data capture (structured fields, clean labels, keep DTC/part numbers) and run this text-mining pipeline weekly as an early-warning system.
Reading noteAll figures are claim counts, not failure rates: the dataset has no production volumes, so a model or plant with more claims may simply have built more vehicles. The dollar figures in section 6 are illustrative assumptions, not values from the data.

2. Understanding the data

Each row is one warranty event: identifiers and dates, vehicle attributes (build plant, make, model), two coded fields (complaint, cause) and three free-text narratives. Six columns (Trigger, Failure Component, Failure Condition, Additional Context, Fix Component, Fix Condition) were provided empty except for five worked examples, which define the target tag schema. All 1,000 vehicles are model year 2020 and were built within five weeks (Aug-Sep 2019), so calendar time and vehicle age move together and the data behaves like a single cohort ageing through its warranty.

Claims ramp up over the first five months in service, plateau at about 58 a month through 2020 and decline during 2021. The median claim arrives 227 days (about 7.5 months) after the vehicle enters service; 43% arrive within 180 days and 75% within a year.

3. From free text to structured tags

The three narratives were treated as one story: the customer's symptom, the technician's diagnosis, and the repair. A transparent lexicon with clause-level pairing produces the same six tag columns as the examples for all claims. Components and conditions are aligned lists (the i-th component pairs with the i-th condition), exactly as in the provided examples, and the label vocabulary reuses theirs (Black Screen, Controls Irresponsive, Inoperative, No Sound, Internal Fault, Malfunction, Replaced, Programmed ...) and extends it with entities seen in the data.

Coverage: 99.6% of claims received at least one failure component/condition pair and 97.9% a fix pair. Triggers (22%) and additional context (35%) are legitimately sparse; claims without one receive the same default labels the examples use. Spanish (4) and French (13) narratives are handled with multilingual cues but excluded from clustering.

4. What fails, and what technicians do about it

4.1 Failure modes

Display black screen is the most-reported failure mode (22% of claims), followed by an inoperative radio (13%), generic radio malfunction, freezing/lag and no sound. The technician's own diagnosis names an internal fault in 33% of claims. By what the customer notices, the radio in general and the display dominate, with camera/video and satellite-radio reception next.

4.2 One repair fits (almost) every symptom

88.3% of claims end with the radio replaced and 66.5% are replaced and reprogrammed; only 7.4% are resolved by reprogramming alone. The share is the same whatever the coded complaint (Audio, Communication, Navigation, Video: 88-91%) and whichever plant built the vehicle. This is why the radio/head-unit is the prime root-cause candidate (hypothesis H1) - and it is also why customer symptoms such as 'camera inoperative' are, for repair purposes, radio failures.

5. Patterns, trends and significant changes

5.1 Volume over time

With the 2020 mean of 58 claims/month as the baseline (Poisson 3-sigma limits 35-81), the only months outside the limits are the ramp-up months (Sep 2019, Oct 2019, Nov 2019, Dec 2019) and May 2021. No month is unusually high: there is no spike, but a gradual age-related rise and fall.

5.2 What is changing - after correcting for multiple testing

We compared the early period (Aug-19 to Mar-20), mid period (Apr-Dec 2020) and 2021 for 32 categories (affected functions, issue types, every failure condition and update-related wording) and corrected for the number of tests (Benjamini-Hochberg). Only one change is significant: Stays On/Battery Drain - 4.0% -> 2.2% -> 11.1% (adjusted p < 0.001). Every other apparent shift is not significant after correction (the next-best has an adjusted p of 0.06); these include the rise of the generic 'radio' share and complaints that mention a software update.

These 46 claims are mostly triggered after ignition-off (57%), arrive later in the vehicle's life (median 309 vs 226 days), are escalated to TAC far less often (13% vs 34%) and are fixed by reprogramming alone more often (11% vs 7%) - a profile consistent with a power-management / sleep-mode software behaviour rather than a hardware failure (hypothesis H7; to be confirmed against software release dates).

5.3 Where and when

  • Plants: no significant association between build plant and affected function (p = 0.57) or issue category (p = 0.67). The radio-replacement rate ranges 83%-94% across plants (p = 0.07, not significant).
  • Models: affected function differs by model (p = 1.2e-05). The strongly over-represented cells that survive a Bonferroni correction are all camera/video: FusionNova 18 claims vs 7 expected (z = 4.8); HyperFury X 30 claims vs 17 expected (z = 3.7); StellarGlide 16 claims vs 7 expected (z = 3.7). At make level, NebulaCruiser is over-represented as well.
  • Time in service does not differ significantly by issue category (Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.12) or function (p = 0.40). 21% of claims occur within 90 days; 76 claims precede the in-service date.

5.4 Clusters, topics and likely root causes

Two unsupervised views complement the rules. Symptom clusters group the customer's wording (K-Means on TF-IDF/LSA, k = 10; and K-Means on all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence embeddings, k = 10). Repair topics (NMF) group the technician narratives. Cluster separation is weak - silhouette 0.10 (TF-IDF), and the TF-IDF solution is only moderately stable across restarts (ARI 0.49) - as is typical for short text, so clusters are used as a lens, and findings are reported only where independent views agree. The embedding clusters match the rule-based functions better (NMI 0.35 vs 0.19 for TF-IDF).

7. Key learnings, limitations and further improvements

What worked

  • Reading the five worked examples carefully defined a usable tag vocabulary; a transparent lexicon with clause pairing reproduced the same six columns for every claim with fully auditable rules.
  • Evidence beat codes. The structured complaint/cause fields did not predict findings or repairs; the narratives did.
  • Statistical discipline kept the story honest. Most tempting patterns (plant differences, time trends, time-in-service by issue) vanished after correction; what remains (stays-on trend, camera/video concentration) is supported by independent views.
  • Cross-method agreement (rules, TF-IDF, sentence embeddings, topic model) gave confidence in the camera/video finding and the radio-as-hub finding.

Limitations - read before acting

  • No denominators: counts, not rates; no costs; no supplier/lot/part numbers; no returned-part teardown results.
  • Tag accuracy is only lightly validated (five labelled rows plus consistency checks); condition tagging is the weakest component.
  • Narratives are truncated and stripped of numbers, DTC codes and part numbers; 'cause not documented' describes the text, not necessarily what the technician knew.
  • One production cohort (MY2020, five-week build window): findings may not generalise.
  • Weak cluster structure: low silhouette scores and moderate stability; clusters are a lens, not hard classes. The software-update-wording rise (p = 0.007) did not survive correction and is exploratory only.

Further improvements

  • Use an open-source LLM with the five examples as few-shot prompts to extract tags, then compare with the lexicon output and keep the union where they agree; use the lexicon output as silver labels.
  • Have domain experts label 200-300 claims, fine-tune a token-classification model and report true precision/recall.
  • Join production volumes, supplier lots and teardown results so rates, supplier effects and true root causes become measurable; add survival / Weibull analysis for warranty-exposure forecasting.
  • Operationalise: run the pipeline weekly with control charts and alerts on tag shares.
